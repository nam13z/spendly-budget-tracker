# Spendly — Product Blueprint

## 1. North star

Spendly helps people make confident everyday money decisions by turning fragmented financial activity into a clear, explainable picture of what they have, what is committed, what is flexible and what needs attention.

**Positioning:** A calm financial command centre for real life — not a spreadsheet, not a bank, and not a guilt machine.

## 2. Problems to solve

Traditional budget trackers commonly create friction in several areas. These are product hypotheses to validate, not claims that every competitor has every flaw.

### 2.1 Tracking becomes another chore
Manual entry, category cleanup and repetitive forms can make users abandon tracking.

**Spendly response:** fast entry, sensible defaults, recent categories, reusable descriptions, bulk editing later, clear recurring rules and a forgiving correction flow.

### 2.2 A balance does not tell the whole story
Money in an account may already be needed for rent, bills, debt payments or goals.

**Spendly response:** distinguish account balance, committed funds, reserved funds, budget availability and Safe-to-Spend.

### 2.3 Charts show what happened but not what to do
A pie chart rarely explains why spending changed or what action is reasonable.

**Spendly response:** contextual insights with comparison period, evidence, caveats and a useful next step.

### 2.4 Real life is not a neat monthly salary
Students, freelancers and households may receive money irregularly or from multiple sources.

**Spendly response:** multiple income sources, expected vs received status, flexible periods and cashflow views.

### 2.5 Shared money is confusing
Household spending, personal purchases, payer identity and who benefited are different concepts.

**Spendly response:** distinct spaces, accounts, payer and allocation records. One purchase remains one actual transaction.

### 2.6 Budgeting can feel punitive
Overspending alerts, broken streaks and shame-based language can make people disengage.

**Spendly response:** neutral language, adjustable plans, recovery workflows and no punitive gamification.

### 2.7 Trust is fragile
Unclear calculations, opaque syncing, invasive permissions or unexplained AI recommendations damage trust.

**Spendly response:** transparent formulas, explicit data controls, manual-first MVP and verified calculation engine.

## 3. Product pillars

1. **Clarity:** show what is true now.
2. **Commitments:** make upcoming obligations visible.
3. **Agency:** let users choose how to budget.
4. **Resilience:** help users adapt when plans change.
5. **Shared understanding:** support household collaboration without erasing privacy.
6. **Trust:** accurate calculations, clear explanations and user control.
7. **Delight:** polished interaction that never interferes with financial tasks.

## 4. Product model

Spendly models several related but distinct concepts:

- **User:** an authenticated person.
- **Household:** a collaboration boundary with members and roles.
- **Space:** an organizational/visibility context such as Home or Personal.
- **Account:** where money is held (cash, bank, savings).
- **Transaction:** a real-world financial event.
- **Allocation:** how a transaction is attributed across spaces/categories/beneficiaries.
- **Budget:** a plan for a period and scope.
- **Commitment:** a future bill or reserved obligation.
- **Goal:** a desired future savings outcome.
- **Insight:** an explanation generated from verified facts.

Do not collapse these concepts into one table or one generic “wallet”.

## 5. Signature experience

### Home overview
Answer, in this order:
1. What is my current position?
2. What is already committed?
3. What is safe to spend?
4. What needs attention soon?
5. What changed since last time?

Do not lead with a wall of charts.

### Quick add
A user should be able to record a straightforward expense in a few focused actions. Progressive disclosure hides optional details until needed. Keyboard and mobile input should both feel natural.

### Financial explanation
Every important derived number should have a “How is this calculated?” explanation. Show included items, excluded items, as-of date and assumptions.

### Recovery
If the user exceeds a budget:
- explain the difference without judgment;
- show whether it affects actual cashflow or only the chosen plan;
- offer options to adjust future allocations, use a buffer or revise the plan;
- do not automatically move money or change budgets.

## 6. Product boundaries

Spendly is not initially:
- a bank;
- a payment processor;
- an investment platform;
- a credit-scoring product;
- a tax/accounting system;
- a financial adviser;
- an AI chatbot.

## 7. Long-term expansion

After core trust and retention are demonstrated:
- offline-first mobile entry and sync;
- AI-generated explanations grounded in calculation results;
- optional bank imports through reputable providers;
- advanced household permissions;
- forecasting and automation;
- native mobile widgets and haptics;
- desktop clients if there is proven demand.

## 8. Product success

North-star outcome: users report that Spendly helps them understand their financial position and make decisions with less effort.

Supporting metrics:
- time to record an expense;
- onboarding completion;
- weekly active tracking users;
- percentage of users who understand Safe-to-Spend;
- budget adjustment and recovery success;
- recurring commitment completion;
- retention by cohort;
- user-reported clarity and trust.

Avoid optimizing for compulsive opens, streaks or transaction volume alone.
